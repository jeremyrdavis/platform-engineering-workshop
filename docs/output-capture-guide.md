## Photography Guidance for Analog Outputs

### Overview

The workshop produces three primary types of analog outputs that must be photographed for preservation and distribution:

1. **Flip Chart Outputs:** Future-state visions, OKRs, marketing strategies (if included)
2. **Post-It Note Clusters:** Platform requirements organized by theme with prioritization dots
3. **Facilitator Synthesis Notes:** Whiteboard or flip chart summaries captured throughout the day

All photography should be completed before participants leave the venue at 5:00 PM.

### General Photography Principles

**Lighting:**
- Ensure adequate overhead and natural lighting on all materials
- Avoid shadows from photographer or equipment
- Use flash if necessary for post-it note close-ups (text must be legible)
- Position flip charts near windows for natural light when possible

**Framing:**
- Shoot flip charts and whiteboards straight-on (perpendicular to surface, not at angle)
- Fill frame with the output (minimize empty space around edges)
- Ensure all text, diagrams, and sticky notes are fully visible in frame
- Avoid cutting off content at edges of photo

**Consistency:**
- Use same device for all photos (consistent quality and file format)
- Use highest resolution settings on camera or smartphone
- Take multiple shots of each output to ensure at least one is clear
- Review photos immediately after capture to verify legibility before moving materials

**Participant Privacy:**
- Avoid including identifiable participant faces unless explicit permission is granted
- Focus on outputs rather than individuals
- If group photos are desired, obtain verbal consent before photographing

### Flip Chart Photography Technique

**Setup:**
1. Position flip chart on stand or secure to wall with tape
2. Ensure chart is flat and not curled at corners
3. Stand 4-6 feet back from flip chart
4. Use portrait orientation for standard flip charts (vertical text layout)

**Composition:**
- Frame entire flip chart including headline and all bullet points
- Ensure margins are visible but minimal (10-15% border around content)
- Verify all handwritten text is in focus before moving to next chart

**Multiple Angles:**
- Take one straight-on shot for documentation
- Take one slightly closer shot if any text is small or light
- If flip chart has dense content, take additional close-up of specific sections

**Lighting Adjustments:**
- If using flash, angle chart slightly to avoid glare on glossy paper
- If natural light creates shadows, reposition chart or photographer
- Test one shot first and adjust lighting before photographing all charts

### Post-It Note Photography Technique

**Challenges:**
- Post-it notes are small (3x3 inches) with varied handwriting legibility
- Clusters may span large wall areas (8-10 feet wide)
- Individual notes may have light or small handwriting

**Wide Shots (Context Photos):**
1. Step back 8-10 feet to capture entire wall of post-its
2. Use landscape orientation
3. Take multiple wide shots showing full context of all clusters
4. Ensure theme labels are visible in wide shots

**Cluster Close-Ups (Detail Photos):**
1. Photograph each thematic cluster individually
2. Frame cluster to include theme label and all post-its in that group
3. Get close enough that post-it text is legible when zoomed
4. Use portrait or landscape orientation based on cluster shape
5. Ensure adequate lighting for text readability
6. Take 2-3 photos per cluster to guarantee clarity

**High-Priority Item Photos:**
1. Identify post-its with 3+ prioritization dots
2. Photograph these individually if needed for clarity
3. Capture post-it with visible dots showing priority

**Technical Tips:**
- Use flash for close-ups if wall area has poor lighting
- Hold camera steady or use two hands to avoid blur
- Focus camera explicitly on text (tap screen to focus on smartphone)
- Review each photo after capture and re-shoot if text is illegible

### Whiteboard and Synthesis Notes Photography

**Facilitator Synthesis:**
- Photograph any whiteboard notes made during synthesis discussions
- Capture parking lot items written on flip charts or whiteboards
- Document any diagrams or frameworks drawn during sessions
- Take close-up photos of participant questions captured on boards

**Process:**
1. Clean or erase any non-relevant content from whiteboard first
2. Ensure markers are dry before photographing (to avoid smearing)
3. Use straight-on framing (perpendicular to board)
4. Increase exposure if whiteboard appears dim in test photo
5. Capture full board first, then take close-ups of specific sections if needed

## Photo Quality Requirements and Technical Specifications

### Minimum Quality Standards

**Resolution:**
- Minimum: 8 megapixels (3264 x 2448 pixels)
- Recommended: 12+ megapixels for best text legibility
- Use highest quality setting on smartphone or camera

**File Format:**
- JPEG format is acceptable (.jpg)
- PNG format preferred for archival (.png)
- Avoid compression if possible (reduces text legibility)

**Focus and Clarity:**
- All text must be legible when viewed at 100% zoom on computer screen
- No motion blur (hold camera steady for 1-2 seconds)
- Proper focus on text content (not background)

**Lighting and Exposure:**
- Text should have high contrast against background
- Avoid overexposure (blown out highlights making text illegible)
- Avoid underexposure (dark photos with unreadable text)
- Test exposure on first photo and adjust before shooting all outputs

### Angle and Perspective Recommendations

**Flip Charts:**
- Straight-on perspective (camera parallel to chart surface)
- Camera height: Center of chart should be at camera center
- Distance: 4-6 feet from chart
- Avoid angled shots that distort text or create keystone effect

**Post-It Note Walls:**
- Perpendicular to wall surface (not at oblique angle)
- For wide shots: Step back far enough to capture full width
- For close-ups: Get close enough for text legibility but avoid shadows
- Take multiple shots at different distances to ensure coverage

**Whiteboards:**
- Perpendicular to board (avoid angle distortion)
- Ensure even lighting across entire board
- Increase camera exposure if board appears dim
- Clean any smudges or old marks before photographing

### Device Recommendations

**Smartphone Photography:**
- Modern smartphones (iPhone 8+, Android equivalent) are sufficient
- Enable HDR mode for even exposure
- Tap screen to focus on text before shooting
- Use grid overlay to ensure straight framing
- Avoid using digital zoom (reduces quality) - step closer instead

**Digital Camera:**
- DSLR or mirrorless camera provides highest quality
- Use auto-focus mode for quick capture
- Set to highest resolution JPEG setting
- Consider using tripod for stability if available

**Tablet Photography:**
- Acceptable but not ideal (harder to hold steady)
- Use if smartphone or camera not available
- Follow same guidelines as smartphone photography

## Post-Workshop Transcription and Digitization

### Transcription Process

**Priority Order for Transcription:**
1. Platform Requirements (post-it notes) - highest priority due to small handwriting
2. OKRs and Goals - measurable key results and targets
3. Future-State Visions - aspirational outcomes and headlines
4. Facilitator synthesis notes and parking lot items
5. Marketing strategy outputs (if activity was conducted)

### Flip Chart Transcription Guidelines

**Format:**
Use structured markdown format to preserve hierarchy and organization:

```markdown
## [Activity Name] - [Group Name]

### [Section Heading or Objective]

- [Bullet point 1]
- [Bullet point 2]
- [Bullet point 3]

### [Next Section]

- [Content...]
```

**Example - OKRs Transcription:**
```markdown
## OKRs Development - Developer Experience Group

### Objective 1: Transform developer onboarding into world-class experience

- Key Result 1: Reduce time-to-first-deployment from 2 weeks to 4 hours
- Key Result 2: Achieve 90% developer satisfaction score for onboarding
- Key Result 3: Enable 100% of new developers to deploy to dev environment on day 1
- Measurement: Developer Hub analytics, onboarding survey (NPS), time-tracking

### Objective 2: [Next objective...]
```

**Transcription Best Practices:**
- Preserve exact wording from flip charts (don't paraphrase or correct)
- Note any illegible text as: [illegible - appears to say "X"]
- Include context notes if abbreviations are unclear
- Maintain original organization and structure
- Add group name or topic to each transcribed section

### Post-It Note Transcription Guidelines

**Format:**
Organize by theme cluster with priority indicators:

```markdown
## Platform Requirements

### Theme: [Cluster Name]

#### Pain Points
- [Post-it text] (Priority: 3 dots)
- [Post-it text] (Priority: 1 dot)
- [Post-it text] (No dots)

#### Desired Capabilities
- [Post-it text] (Priority: 5 dots)
- [Post-it text] (Priority: 2 dots)
```

**Example - Requirements Transcription:**
```markdown
## Platform Requirements

### Theme: CI/CD Pipeline & Automation

#### Pain Points
- Waiting 3 days for environment provisioning (Priority: 5 dots)
- Manual security scanning delays releases (Priority: 3 dots)
- No visibility into deployment pipeline status (Priority: 2 dots)
- Inconsistent configurations across environments (Priority: 1 dot)

#### Desired Capabilities
- Self-service environment creation in <5 min (Priority: 7 dots)
- Automated security scanning in CI pipeline (Priority: 4 dots)
- Real-time deployment dashboard with logs (Priority: 3 dots)
- GitOps-based config management (Priority: 1 dot)
```

**Transcription Challenges:**
- **Illegible Handwriting:** Note as [illegible - theme: CI/CD] to preserve count
- **Duplicate Ideas:** Keep all instances, note as "(Duplicate mentioned by multiple participants)"
- **Ambiguous Placement:** If post-it could fit multiple themes, note both: "(Also relevant to [Other Theme])"
- **Priority Dots:** Count carefully in photos, note total dots per item

**Tools for Transcription:**
- Use photo viewing software with zoom capability
- Work from close-up cluster photos for best text legibility
- Cross-reference wide shots to verify cluster organization
- Consider using OCR (Optical Character Recognition) tools as starting point, but verify accuracy

## Real-Time Note-Taking and Scribing

### Designated Scribe Role

**Option 1: Co-Facilitator as Scribe**
- Assign a second facilitator whose primary role is documentation
- Scribe takes notes on laptop during all sessions
- Captures key discussion points, questions, and insights in real-time
- Does not lead sessions but may support with logistics

**Option 2: Participant Volunteer as Scribe**
- Request a participant volunteer to serve as note-taker
- Provide laptop and note-taking template
- Scribe still participates in activities but captures discussion highlights
- Rotate scribe role every 2 hours to share responsibility

**Option 3: Facilitator Self-Documentation**
- Primary facilitator takes brief notes during transitions and breaks
- Captures synthesis points on whiteboard/flip chart as discussion happens
- Photographs notes immediately after each session
- Transcribes and expands notes within 24 hours while memory is fresh

### Note-Taking Template

**Real-Time Notes Format:**

```markdown
# Platform Engineering Workshop - Real-Time Notes
Date: [Date]
Participants: [Count and organizations represented]
Facilitator: [Name]
Scribe: [Name]

---

## Session: [Session Name]
Time: [Start Time] - [End Time]

### Key Discussion Points
- [Point 1]
- [Point 2]
- [Point 3]

### Insights and Aha Moments
- [Participant comment or realization]
- [Unexpected connection or theme]

### Questions Raised
- [Question 1] - [Assigned to parking lot / Answered during session]
- [Question 2]

### Action Items or Follow-Ups
- [Item requiring post-workshop follow-up]

---

[Repeat for each session]
```

### What to Capture in Real-Time Notes

**During Presentations:**
- Key questions from participants (especially recurring themes)
- Examples or stories shared by participants
- Concepts that generated strong reactions or engagement
- Technical details or vendor-specific questions requiring follow-up

**During Interactive Activities:**
- Participant insights during report-outs
- Patterns or themes facilitator observes across groups
- Creative or novel approaches groups develop
- Challenges or objections raised during activities
- Quotes that capture participant sentiment or breakthroughs

**During Discussions and Synthesis:**
- Common themes emerging across activities
- Divergent viewpoints or healthy debates
- Connections participants make between concepts
- Commitments or next steps volunteers verbalize

**Do NOT Capture:**
- Verbatim transcripts of everything said (unsustainable)
- Detailed presentation content (already in slides)
- Every individual contribution (focus on themes and highlights)

### Note-Taking Best Practices

**During Workshop:**
- Use shorthand and abbreviations for speed
- Focus on insights and themes, not transcription
- Mark follow-up items with "ACTION:" prefix for easy identification
- Note the speaker for important quotes: "[Name]: 'quote here'"
- Use timestamps for key moments (helps correlate with photos later)

**Post-Workshop Processing:**
- Expand abbreviated notes within 24 hours while memory is fresh
- Cross-reference notes with photos of flip charts and post-its
- Organize notes by session matching workshop schedule
- Identify recurring themes across multiple sessions
- Flag items requiring facilitator follow-up or clarification

## Parking Lot and Follow-Up Question Capture

### Parking Lot Purpose and Setup

**What is the Parking Lot?**
A designated flip chart or whiteboard where the facilitator captures:
- Questions that arise during presentations but are tangential to current discussion
- Complex technical questions requiring research or vendor consultation
- Topics participants want to discuss but are out of scope for current session
- Ideas or suggestions that don't fit current activity but are valuable

**Physical Setup:**
- Dedicate one flip chart stand with header "Parking Lot - Questions & Ideas"
- Position near front of room but off to side (visible but not central focus)
- Keep markers accessible for facilitator to quickly add items
- Leave flip chart up throughout entire workshop

### When to Use Parking Lot

**During Presentations:**
- Participant asks detailed technical question about future topic: "Great question - let me add that to parking lot and we'll address it during the [session name] discussion."
- Question requires research or follow-up: "I don't have that specific data point - let me capture that for follow-up after the workshop."
- Discussion goes off-topic: "This is a valuable conversation - let me note it on the parking lot so we stay on schedule, and we can return to it during breaks or closing."

**During Activities:**
- Group identifies requirement outside platform scope: "That's more about organizational change than platform tooling - let's capture it on parking lot for separate discussion."
- Participant has idea for workshop format improvement: "I like that idea for future workshops - let me note it on the parking lot."

**When NOT to Use Parking Lot:**
- Questions directly relevant to current discussion (answer them now)
- Quick clarification questions (answer immediately)
- Critical blockers to current activity (address now)
- When used too frequently, it frustrates participants (shows facilitator is dismissive)

### Parking Lot Format

**Simple List Format:**
```
PARKING LOT - Questions & Ideas

• What specific DORA metrics does Developer Hub track? [Technical Detail]
• How do we handle security team objections to self-service? [Follow-Up]
• Can we integrate platform with existing ServiceNow ticketing? [Vendor Question]
• Idea: Include FinOps cost dashboards in future workshop [Future Enhancement]
• Need to research policy-as-code tools for regulated industries [Action Item]
```

**Categories (Optional):**
If parking lot grows long, group items:
- [TECH] = Technical detail questions
- [FOLLOW-UP] = Requires post-workshop research
- [SCOPE] = Out of scope for today but valuable
- [IDEA] = Enhancement suggestion

### Processing Parking Lot Items

**During Workshop:**
- Review parking lot during breaks and identify items that can be quickly addressed
- Bring up relevant parking lot items during later sessions: "Earlier someone asked about [topic] - this slide addresses that."
- Check in on parking lot during closing synthesis: "Let's review what we captured on parking lot."

**During Closing Session (4:50-5:00 PM):**
- Briefly review parking lot items with participants
- Categorize items: "These three will be addressed in post-workshop follow-up. This one we can answer now. This one requires vendor consultation."
- Capture parking lot items in post-workshop deliverables

**Post-Workshop:**
- Include all parking lot items in "Follow-Up Questions" section of deliverables
- Research answers to technical questions and provide in follow-up email
- Connect participants with appropriate resources or vendor contacts
- Schedule follow-up call if parking lot reveals significant unaddressed needs

### Follow-Up Question Documentation Template

**Include in Post-Workshop Deliverables:**

```markdown
# Follow-Up Questions and Answers
## From Platform Engineering Workshop - [Date]

### Questions Raised During Workshop

**Q: [Question from parking lot]**
A: [Answer provided by facilitator or researched post-workshop]

**Q: [Next question]**

  - Resources: [Who to contact, what to read]

- [Topic] - Recommend separate workshop or working group
- [Topic] - Suggest 1:1 consultation with [Red Hat specialist]
```

## Backup Strategies for Photo Capture Failures

### Preventing Photo Capture Failures

**Pre-Workshop Preparation:**
- Charge camera/smartphone battery fully before workshop
- Clear storage space on device (ensure room for 100+ photos)
- Test camera functionality and settings day before workshop
- Have backup smartphone or camera available
- Bring charging cable and portable battery pack

**During Workshop:**
- Review photos immediately after each activity to verify quality
- Re-take any blurry or illegible photos before moving materials
- Backup photos to cloud storage during lunch break (Google Photos, Dropbox, etc.)
- Email critical photos to yourself as additional backup

### If Camera Fails During Workshop

**Immediate Backup Options:**

1. **Use Participant Smartphone:**
   - Request a participant volunteer to photograph outputs using their device
   - Have participant email or AirDrop photos to facilitator immediately
   - Provide specific instructions for framing and lighting

2. **Borrow Device:**
   - Borrow smartphone from venue staff or co-facilitator
   - Use tablet if no other option available
   - Use venue's camera if professional photographer is available

3. **Delay Material Cleanup:**
   - Do not remove flip charts or post-its from walls
   - Leave all outputs in place until photos are captured
   - Return after acquiring working camera (same day if possible)
   - Inform participants that outputs will be photographed after workshop

### If Photos Are Lost or Corrupted

**Mitigation Strategies:**

1. **Leverage Real-Time Notes:**
   - Use scribe notes to reconstruct key outputs
   - Reference facilitator synthesis notes on whiteboard (photograph these!)
   - Rely on transcriptions if any were started during workshop

2. **Participant Reconstruction:**
   - Email participants requesting their personal photos if they took any
   - Ask groups to briefly recreate their flip chart outputs from memory
   - Schedule 30-minute follow-up call to verbally review outputs

3. **Partial Recovery:**
   - Identify which photos are still intact
   - Prioritize re-creating lost outputs by importance:
     - Priority 1: Platform Requirements (most actionable)
     - Priority 2: OKRs (measurable commitments)
     - Priority 3: Future-State Visions (aspirational context)

### If Materials Are Damaged or Destroyed

**Post-It Notes Fall Off Wall:**
- Prevention: Test post-it adhesion before workshop, use painter's tape backup
- If fallen: Photograph floor layout immediately before moving, use facilitator memory and notes to reconstruct clusters
- Partial reconstruction: Note which items are lost: "[Cluster had approximately 8 additional post-its - content not recovered]"

**Flip Charts Damaged:**
- Prevention: Secure with masking tape, avoid placing near high-traffic areas
- If damaged: Photograph remaining visible content, use group memory to reconstruct
- Contact group participants for their notes or memories

**Whiteboard Accidentally Erased:**
- Prevention: Photograph whiteboard notes immediately after capturing, mark whiteboard "DO NOT ERASE" with tape
- If erased: Rely on scribe notes and facilitator memory to reconstruct
- Note in deliverables: "Parking lot items partially reconstructed from notes"

### Backup Photo Storage

**During Workshop:**
- Upload photos to cloud storage during lunch break (Google Drive, Dropbox, iCloud)
- Email critical photos to yourself or co-facilitator
- Use automatic photo backup on smartphone if enabled

**Post-Workshop:**
- Immediately backup all photos to computer hard drive
- Store copies in at least two locations (cloud + external drive)
- Rename files with descriptive names before organizing
- Do not delete photos from camera until deliverables are distributed

**Move photos into appropriate folders during lunch and afternoon break:**
- Prevents overwhelming mass of unsorted photos at end of day
- Easier to identify missing coverage while workshop is still in session
- Reduces post-workshop processing time

### Post-Workshop Processing Workflow

**Within 24 Hours:**

1. **Review and Cull:**
   - Review all photos and select best shot of each output
   - Delete obvious duplicates or failed shots (blurry, dark, cut off)
   - Move alternate shots to "Extras-Alternates" folder (don't delete yet)

2. **Rename Files:**
   - Apply consistent naming convention to all files
   - Ensure file names are descriptive enough to identify content without opening

3. **Organize by Organization (if multiple orgs):**
   - Create separate folders for each participating organization
   - Copy only their specific outputs to their folder
   - Shared activities (full group) go in all organization folders

4. **Backup:**
   - Upload to cloud storage (Google Drive, Dropbox)
   - Copy to external hard drive
   - Keep originals on computer hard drive
   - Verify backups are accessible before deleting any originals

**Within 3 Days:**

5. **Transcribe Outputs:**
   - Use organized photos to transcribe flip charts and post-its
   - Create markdown files for each activity
   - Save transcriptions in deliverables folders

6. **Synthesize Action Items:**
   - Review all photos and transcriptions
   - Identify top priorities and themes
   - Create synthesized action items document

7. **Create Deliverables Packages:**
   - Assemble complete folder structure for each organization
   - Export transcriptions to PDF
   - Write README file explaining contents
   - Test shared links to ensure accessibility

**Within 5 Days:**

8. **Distribute Deliverables:**
   - Send email to each organization with their deliverables link
   - Include brief summary of workshop outcomes
   - Offer follow-up call or consultation
   - Request feedback on deliverables format and content
