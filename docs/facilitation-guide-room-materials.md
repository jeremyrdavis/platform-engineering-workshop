## Room Configuration and Setup

### Overview and Design Principles

The workshop requires a flexible room that can support multiple activity formats throughout the day. Based on the visual evidence from workshop photos (IMG_1916.png, IMG_1918.png), successful room configurations enable:

- **Seated presentations** with clear sightlines to projector/screen
- **Small group table work** for collaborative flip chart exercises
- **Standing wall/window activities** for post-it note brainstorming and affinity mapping
- **Easy transitions** between seated and standing formats
- **Simultaneous activities** where some groups work at tables while others work at walls

### Participant Count and Table Arrangement

**For 10-12 Participants:**
- **Table Configuration:** 2 rectangular tables or 2 round tables
- **Seating:** 5-6 people per table
- **Group Formation:** 2 groups for breakout activities
- **Space Needed:** Minimum 400 square feet

**For 13-16 Participants:**
- **Table Configuration:** 3 rectangular tables or 3 round tables
- **Seating:** 4-5 people per table
- **Group Formation:** 3 groups for breakout activities
- **Space Needed:** Minimum 500 square feet

**For 17-20 Participants:**
- **Table Configuration:** 4 rectangular tables or 4 round tables
- **Seating:** 4-5 people per table
- **Group Formation:** 4 groups for breakout activities
- **Space Needed:** Minimum 600 square feet

### Table Specifications and Arrangement

**Recommended Table Types:**
- **Rectangular Tables:** 6-8 feet long, 3-4 feet wide (preferred for flip chart work)
- **Round Tables:** 5-6 feet diameter (better for discussion, less space for materials)
- **Height:** Standard table height (28-30 inches) to accommodate seated work

**Table Positioning:**
1. **Presentation Mode (9:00-11:15 AM, 11:30 AM-12:00 PM, 2:10-3:15 PM):**
   - Arrange tables in classroom-style or chevron pattern facing screen
   - Ensure all seats have clear sightlines to presentation screen
   - Leave center aisle for facilitator movement and energy
   - Position tables 8-10 feet from screen minimum

2. **Collaborative Mode (1:00-2:00 PM, 3:30-4:30 PM):**
   - Tables can remain in same position for seated flip chart work
   - Ensure adequate space around each table for facilitator circulation
   - Keep tables close enough to walls for standing transition
   - Allow 3-4 feet between table groupings for movement

**Table Setup Best Practices:**
- Place power outlets accessible to each table (for laptops)
- Ensure room lighting illuminates table surfaces well
- Avoid placing tables directly under air vents or bright lights
- Keep emergency exits and paths clear per fire code

### Wall and Window Space Requirements

**Critical for Post-It Note Exercise (3:30-4:30 PM):**

**Minimum Wall/Window Space:**
- **For 10 participants:** 8 feet wide x 4 feet high
- **For 15 participants:** 10 feet wide x 4 feet high
- **For 20 participants:** 12 feet wide x 5 feet high

**Ideal Wall/Window Characteristics:**
- **Surface:** Glass windows or smooth painted walls work best for post-it adhesion
- **Location:** Visible from participant seating area
- **Height:** Participants should reach comfortably (4-6 feet from floor)
- **Accessibility:** Space for 10-20 people to stand simultaneously without crowding
- **Lighting:** Well-lit for photography and text legibility

**Testing Wall Surface:**
- Test post-it note adhesion 1 week before workshop
- If post-its don't stick well, prepare masking tape or painter's tape
- Alternative: Use large foam boards or poster boards mounted on wall

**Designating Wall Areas:**
Before workshop (during setup), mark wall sections with painter's tape:
- Section 1: Pain Points (left side, ~40% of space)
- Section 2: Desired Capabilities (right side, ~40% of space)
- Section 3: Clustering Area (center, ~20% of space for affinity mapping)

### Flip Chart Stand Positioning

**Required Stands:** Minimum 2-3 stands (ideally 1 per breakout group)

**For Different Participant Counts:**
- **10-12 participants (2 groups):** 2 flip chart stands minimum
- **13-16 participants (3 groups):** 3 flip chart stands minimum
- **17-20 participants (4 groups):** 4 flip chart stands ideal (3 minimum with rotation)

**Positioning During Activities:**

**Future-State Visioning (9:00 AM):**
- Place stands near each table group for easy access
- Space stands 6-8 feet apart so groups can work independently
- Ensure all stands are visible from facilitator position
- Position close to walls if flip charts will be displayed later

**OKRs Activity (1:00 PM):**
- Keep stands near table groups
- Ensure markers and materials are within arm's reach
- Allow space for participants to stand at flip charts while group mates remain seated

**After Activities (for display):**
- Move completed flip charts to front of room or along walls
- Display at eye level (5-6 feet from floor) for visibility
- Use masking tape to secure to walls if removing from stands
- Arrange in presentation order for group sharing

**Flip Chart Stand Specifications:**
- Standard tripod or H-frame stands
- Adjustable height (should reach 5-6 feet when extended)
- Stable base to prevent tipping during active use
- Clips or clamps to secure flip chart pad

### Projector and Screen Setup

**Equipment Requirements:**
- High-quality projector (minimum 3000 lumens for rooms with windows)
- Screen size: Minimum 6 feet diagonal (larger for rooms over 500 sq ft)
- HDMI and VGA cables for laptop connection
- Backup dongle for Mac/PC compatibility
- Remote clicker for presenter (wireless presentation remote)

**Positioning:**
- Screen at front of room, centered for classroom-style seating
- Screen height: Bottom edge 4-5 feet from floor (above heads of seated participants)
- Projector distance: Follow manufacturer's throw ratio for screen size
- Avoid placement where windows create screen glare

**Testing:**
- Test projection 1 hour before workshop starts
- Verify image clarity from all seating positions
- Check audio if videos will be shown
- Have backup laptop in case of technical issues

### Lighting Considerations

**Dual Requirements - Presentation vs. Collaboration:**

**During Presentations:**
- Dim overhead lights near screen to reduce glare
- Maintain ambient lighting so participants can take notes
- Use window blinds/shades to control natural light on screen
- Ensure facilitator can be seen (don't go completely dark)

**During Collaborative Activities:**
- Full lighting for table work (reading, writing on flip charts)
- Bright lighting on wall areas for post-it note exercises
- Adequate lighting for photography of outputs
- Natural light is ideal for energy, but control to avoid glare

**Lighting Controls:**
- Test dimmer switches or lighting zones before workshop
- Identify which lights affect screen vs. work areas
- Assign someone to adjust lighting during transitions
- Have flashlight or phone light backup for photography

### Space for Seated-to-Standing Transitions

**Critical Transition Points:**
- After OKRs activity → Developer Hub demo (seated to seated, 10 min)
- After Platform Capabilities presentation → Platform Requirements activity (seated to standing, 15 min)

**Design Considerations:**

**Clear Pathways:**
- Ensure 3-4 foot clear pathways from each table to wall area
- Remove obstacles (bags, coats, extra chairs) from floor during breaks
- Mark clear path with tape if room is crowded

**Standing Space at Walls:**
- Minimum 6 feet of depth in front of wall/window area
- Participants should stand comfortably without touching tables behind them
- Allow participants to step back to view full wall during clustering
- Ensure wheelchair accessibility to wall space

**Quick Transition Setup:**
- During mid-afternoon break (3:15-3:30 PM), prepare wall area
- Distribute post-its and markers on tables before break
- Have facilitator demonstrate standing at wall during transition
- Encourage participants to stand and stretch before wall activity

### Room Reconfiguration During the Day

**Minimal Reconfiguration Needed:**
The workshop is designed to minimize room changes. Tables remain in place throughout the day. Only wall space needs preparation during breaks.

**Key Reconfiguration Moments:**

**Mid-Morning Break (11:15-11:30 AM):**
- No reconfiguration needed
- Photograph future-state vision flip charts
- Prepare OKRs materials for afternoon

**Lunch Break (12:00-1:00 PM):**
- No reconfiguration needed
- Set up OKRs flip chart stands near tables
- Distribute OKR worksheets at each seat
- Prepare example OKR flip chart for display

**Mid-Afternoon Break (3:15-3:30 PM):**
- **Critical setup time for Platform Requirements activity**
- Mark wall sections with tape if not already done
- Distribute post-it notes (3 colors) on each table
- Place fine-tip markers at each table (1 per participant)
- Clear floor space in front of wall
- Have dot stickers ready for prioritization phase
- Test camera/phone for photography

### Accessibility Considerations

**Wheelchair Access:**
- Ensure table height accommodates wheelchairs (standard height works)
- Leave 36-inch minimum pathways between tables
- Wall/window activities should be accessible (lower height if needed)
- Provide alternative participation modes (writing at table vs. standing at wall)

**Visual Accommodations:**
- Large font on slides (minimum 24pt)
- High contrast flip chart markers (black, dark blue, avoid yellow)
- Adequate lighting for reading materials
- Provide printed materials for those who can't see screen well

**Hearing Accommodations:**
- Microphone for facilitator if room is large or acoustics poor
- Face participants when speaking (not screen)
- Repeat questions from participants for full group

**Mobility Accommodations:**
- Offer seated alternatives for standing activities
- Bring materials to participants if needed
- Extended time for transitions if needed

## Post-It Note Exercise Setup and Facilitation

### Overview

The Platform Requirements Gathering activity (3:30-4:30 PM) is a high-energy, wall-based exercise using post-it notes. This section provides step-by-step setup instructions, distribution guidance, affinity mapping techniques, and troubleshooting tips.

**Activity Format:** Individual brainstorming → collaborative clustering → prioritization voting

### Step-by-Step Wall/Window Setup

**One Week Before Workshop:**

1. **Identify Wall/Window Space:**
   - Visit venue and identify suitable wall or window area
   - Measure space: need minimum 8-10 feet wide, 4-5 feet high
   - Check surface type: glass windows ideal, smooth painted walls good, textured walls problematic

2. **Test Post-It Adhesion:**
   - Bring sample post-it notes to venue
   - Stick several post-its to selected wall/window surface
   - Wait 30 minutes and check if they stay adhered
   - Test in different areas (temperature and surface cleanliness vary)
   - If post-its fall, plan to use masking tape or alternative surface

3. **Plan Camera Angles:**
   - Stand where facilitator will take photos
   - Verify adequate lighting on wall area
   - Check for glare or reflections (especially on windows)
   - Identify best angle for photographing full wall and close-ups

**Day of Workshop - During Mid-Afternoon Break (3:15-3:30 PM):**

1. **Mark Wall Sections (5 minutes):**
   - Use painter's tape to create visual divisions on wall:
     - Left section (40% of space): Label "PAIN POINTS" with pink/red post-it
     - Right section (40% of space): Label "DESIRED CAPABILITIES" with blue/green post-it
     - Center section (20% of space): Leave unlabeled for clustering
   - Tape should be visible but not dominate wall
   - Alternative: Use large post-its as section headers instead of tape

2. **Prepare Floor Space (3 minutes):**
   - Move chairs back from wall area if needed
   - Create standing room for 10-20 participants
   - Ensure 6 feet depth in front of wall minimum
   - Remove obstacles from floor (bags, coats, cables)
   - Mark boundaries with tape if needed for traffic flow

3. **Distribute Materials on Tables (3 minutes):**
   - Place stack of pink/red post-its at each table (pain points)
   - Place stack of blue/green post-its at each table (desired capabilities)
   - Place 1-2 fine-tip markers per participant at each table
   - Keep yellow post-its with facilitator for theme labeling

4. **Prepare Prioritization Materials (2 minutes):**
   - Have dot stickers ready in facilitator area
   - Count out 5 dots per participant in advance
   - Alternative: Have participants use markers to draw dots

5. **Test Lighting and Camera (2 minutes):**
   - Turn on full room lights
   - Take test photo of empty wall to check lighting
   - Adjust window blinds if glare is issue
   - Have camera/phone fully charged and ready

### Distributing Post-It Notes

**Color-Coding Strategy:**

- **Pink or Red:** Pain points, problems, current frustrations
  - Psychological association: red = stop, problems, alert
  - Visual distinction makes clustering easier later

- **Blue or Green:** Desired capabilities, solutions, future needs
  - Psychological association: green = go, growth, positive
  - Creates visual contrast with pain points

- **Yellow:** Theme labels (facilitator use only)
  - Highly visible for category headers
  - Differentiates organizational structure from content

**Quantities Per Participant:**

- **Pain Points (Pink/Red):** 10-15 post-its per participant
  - Expected usage: 5-8 post-its per participant
  - Extras allow for mistakes and rewrites

- **Desired Capabilities (Blue/Green):** 10-15 post-its per participant
  - Expected usage: 5-8 post-its per participant
  - Extras allow for iteration

**Distribution Method:**

1. **During Break (Before Activity Starts):**
   - Place stacks of colored post-its on each table
   - Don't hand out individually (slows down activity start)
   - Participants grab post-its as needed

2. **Facilitator Instructions at Activity Start:**
   - "You'll see pink and blue post-its at your table"
   - "Use pink for pain points - current problems and frustrations"
   - "Use blue for desired capabilities - what you wish the platform could do"
   - "Write one idea per post-it, and be specific"
   - "Grab as many post-its as you need - we have plenty"

3. **During Activity:**
   - Circulate and replenish post-its if tables run low
   - Encourage participants to take more if needed
   - Monitor for waste (participants taking too many at once)

### Affinity Mapping and Clustering Facilitation Techniques

**What is Affinity Mapping:**
Affinity mapping is a collaborative technique where participants organize individual ideas (on post-its) into logical groups or themes. It's particularly effective for finding patterns in brainstorming outputs.

**Phase 1: Individual Brainstorming (Minutes 5-35 of Activity)**

**Setup:**
- Participants work silently at their seats
- Write pain points on pink post-its (minutes 5-20)
- Write desired capabilities on blue post-its (minutes 20-35)
- Stand and place post-its on wall in designated sections as they complete them

**Facilitator Role:**
- Circulate among participants
- Encourage specificity: "Can you add more detail? What exactly takes too long?"
- Point to good examples on wall: "See how this post-it is specific? Aim for that."
- Remind quiet participants to keep writing
- Give time warnings: "10 minutes left for pain points"

**Good vs. Weak Post-Its:**
- **Good:** "Waiting 3 days for AWS account provisioning"
- **Weak:** "Slow provisioning" (not specific enough)
- **Good:** "Manual security review delays release by 2 weeks"
- **Weak:** "Security problems" (too vague)

**Phase 2: Silent Clustering (Minutes 35-42 of Activity)**

**Transition Announcement:**
"Now we'll organize these ideas into themes. Start moving related post-its together. No talking yet - just move post-its silently. Similar ideas should be near each other."

**Facilitator Role:**
1. **Model the Behavior:**
   - Be first to stand at wall
   - Move 2-3 post-its together to show clustering
   - Step back to let participants take over

2. **Observe Patterns:**
   - Watch which clusters naturally emerge
   - Note where participants disagree on placement
   - Identify orphan post-its that don't fit themes

3. **Gentle Guidance:**
   - If cluster gets too large (>15 post-its), suggest split: "This cluster is getting big - could we split into two themes?"
   - If participant seems uncertain, ask: "Where do you think this belongs?"
   - If post-it could fit multiple clusters: "That's fine - it might span two themes"

4. **Encourage Movement:**
   - "Everyone should be up and moving post-its"
   - Point to participants still seated: "Join us at the wall"
   - Keep energy high with gentle urgency

**Silent Clustering Rationale:**
- Silence prevents dominant voices from controlling organization
- Allows for organic pattern recognition
- Participants observe and learn from each other's categorization
- Creates focus and reduces side conversations

**Phase 3: Theme Labeling (Minutes 42-48 of Activity)**

**Transition:**
Once clusters stabilize (most post-its haven't moved in 2-3 minutes), move to theme labeling.

**Facilitator Actions:**
1. **Identify Stable Clusters:**
   - Step back and observe full wall
   - Count clusters (should be 6-10 themes typically)

2. **Create Theme Labels:**
   - Use yellow post-its to write theme names
   - Place theme labels above or beside each cluster
   - Write in large, bold letters for visibility

3. **Collaborative Naming:**
   - Ask group: "What should we call this cluster?"
   - Use participant language (not facilitator jargon)
   - Keep names concise (2-4 words maximum)

**Common Platform Requirement Themes:**
- CI/CD & Deployment Automation
- Environment Provisioning & Management
- Security, Compliance & Policy
- Observability, Logging & Monitoring
- Developer Onboarding & Documentation
- Service Catalog & Discovery
- Testing & Quality Automation
- Infrastructure as Code
- Cost Management & Optimization
- Developer Tooling & IDEs

**Phase 4: Refinement and Review (Minutes 48-50 of Activity)**

**Facilitator-Led Review:**
1. **Full Group Step Back:**
   - "Everyone step back and look at the full wall"
   - "Does anything feel misplaced?"

2. **Allow Final Adjustments:**
   - Participants can move outlier post-its
   - Consolidate very small clusters (2-3 post-its) into larger themes
   - Split overly large clusters if needed

3. **Handle Edge Cases:**
   - Create "Other" or "Cross-Cutting Concerns" cluster for items that don't fit
   - Note duplicates and group together
   - Clarify ambiguous post-its with full group

### Managing Participant Movement

**Encouraging Movement from Seats to Walls:**

**Challenge:** Participants may be hesitant to stand and engage with wall, especially after sitting all afternoon.

**Strategies:**

1. **Explicit Invitation:**
   - "Everyone up! We're going to be standing for this activity."
   - Stand first and gesture to wall energetically
   - Use body language to invite (wave participants forward)

2. **Model the Behavior:**
   - Facilitator stands at wall immediately
   - Place first few post-its on wall yourself
   - Demonstrate standing, reading others' post-its, moving them

3. **Make it Safe:**
   - "There's no wrong way to organize these"
   - "We're all learning from each other"
   - "You can change your mind - just move the post-it"

4. **Address Specific Participants:**
   - Gently encourage quiet participants: "I'd love to see your ideas on the wall"
   - If someone remains seated: "Would you like to join us at the wall, or would you prefer to add post-its from your seat?" (offer options)

**During Clustering Phase:**

1. **Encourage Equal Participation:**
   - "Make sure everyone is touching post-its, not just watching"
   - Redirect if one person dominates: "Let's see what others think about this grouping"

2. **Facilitate Disagreements:**
   - If two participants move same post-it to different clusters: "Both placements make sense - where do you both think it fits best?"
   - Facilitator can make final call if needed: "Let's put it here for now"

3. **Manage Energy:**
   - Keep energy high with movement: "Keep moving post-its around"
   - Give time warnings: "5 minutes left for clustering"
   - Celebrate progress: "Great - I'm seeing clear themes emerge"

### Timing Guidance for Each Phase

**Individual Reflection Phase (15 minutes for pain points, 15 minutes for capabilities):**
- **Minutes 0-5:** Silent brainstorming, intense focus
- **Minutes 5-10:** Participants start standing to place post-its on wall
- **Minutes 10-15:** Continued writing and wall placement
- **Facilitator Time Warnings:**
  - At 10 minutes: "Five minutes left for pain points"
  - At 13 minutes: "Two minutes left - finish up your current thoughts"
  - At 15 minutes: "Let's switch to desired capabilities now"

**Clustering Phase (15 minutes total):**
- **Minutes 0-7:** Silent collaborative clustering
- **Minutes 7-12:** Theme labeling
- **Minutes 12-15:** Refinement and review
- **Facilitator Time Warnings:**
  - At 10 minutes into clustering: "Let's start adding theme labels"
  - At 13 minutes: "Two minutes to finalize clusters"

**Prioritization Phase (10 minutes):**
- **Minutes 0-5:** Distribute dots, participants vote
- **Minutes 5-8:** Step back and observe results
- **Minutes 8-10:** Facilitator synthesis and discussion

### Troubleshooting Tips

**Challenge: Post-Its Falling Off Wall**

**Causes:**
- Poor quality post-its with weak adhesive
- Wall surface not suitable (textured, dirty, or porous)
- Temperature too hot or humid in room
- Post-its have been handled too much (oils from hands reduce stick)

**Solutions:**
- **Immediate Fix:** Use small pieces of masking tape to reinforce post-its
- **Prevention:** Test post-it adhesion on wall one week before workshop
- **Backup Plan:** Prepare foam boards or poster boards as alternative surface
- **Alternative:** Use large sticky flip chart sheets on wall, write directly with markers
- **During Activity:** Assign one participant as "post-it monitor" to re-stick fallen notes

**Challenge: Participants Dominating Clustering**

**Signs:**
- One or two people moving most post-its
- Other participants standing back watching
- Dominant person explaining their reasoning instead of silent clustering

**Solutions:**
- **Reinforce Silent Clustering:** "Remember, no talking during clustering - just move post-its"
- **Direct Invitation:** Point to quieter participants: "What do you think about this grouping?"
- **Redirect Dominant Person:** "Thanks for those moves - let's see what others think now"
- **Rotate Roles:** "Everyone should move at least 5 post-its right now"
- **Physical Positioning:** Stand near dominant person to gently limit their activity

**Challenge: Too Many Post-Its (Overwhelming Volume)**

**Signs:**
- 200+ post-its on wall
- Participants can't read them all
- Clustering becomes chaotic
- Running out of time

**Solutions:**
- **Limit Brainstorming Time:** Enforce strict time limits (15 min pain points, 15 min capabilities)
- **Encourage Consolidation:** During clustering, combine duplicate or very similar post-its
- **Focus on Top Items:** During prioritization, use more dots and focus on highest priorities
- **Extended Time:** If necessary, add 10 minutes to activity (reduce closing session time)

**Challenge: Too Few Post-Its (Low Engagement)**

**Signs:**
- Fewer than 50 post-its total
- Large blank spaces on wall
- Participants finishing too quickly

**Solutions:**
- **Extended Brainstorming:** Give extra 5 minutes for more ideas
- **Prompt with Questions:** Circulate and ask discussion prompts (see facilitation guide)
- **Reference Earlier Work:** "Look at your future-state visions and OKRs - what capabilities would support those?"
- **Encourage Specificity:** "Break that big idea into smaller, specific capabilities"
- **It's Okay:** Sometimes fewer, high-quality post-its is better than quantity

**Challenge: Unclear or Vague Post-Its**

**Examples:**
- "Better tools" (what tools? better how?)
- "Faster" (faster what? how much faster?)
- "Fix deployments" (what specifically is broken?)

**Solutions:**
- **During Brainstorming:** Circulate and coach for specificity
  - Ask: "Can you add more detail to this one?"
  - Show examples of specific post-its
- **During Clustering:** If post-it is too vague to categorize, ask participant to clarify
- **Accept Some Vagueness:** Don't let perfection block progress - capture what you can

**Challenge: Disagreement About Clustering**

**Scenario:**
- Two participants keep moving same post-it to different clusters
- Tension or frustration building

**Solutions:**
- **Acknowledge Both Perspectives:** "This could fit in both themes - that's okay"
- **Create New Theme:** If multiple post-its don't fit, create new cluster
- **Facilitator Decision:** "Let's put it here for now - we can discuss during synthesis"
- **Participant Vote:** Quick hand raise: "Who thinks it belongs in CI/CD? Who thinks Security?"
- **Cross-Cutting:** Some capabilities span themes - note that in synthesis

**Challenge: Activity Running Long**

**Time Pressure:**
- Clustering taking longer than planned
- Need to finish by 4:30 PM for marketing presentation

**Solutions:**
- **Reduce Prioritization Time:** Give 3 dots instead of 5 per participant
- **Skip Full Discussion:** Take photos, promise to synthesize and share post-workshop
- **Facilitator Clusters:** If participants are stuck, facilitator rapidly organizes remaining post-its
- **Extend Slightly:** Add 5-10 minutes, reduce closing session time
- **Continue During Break:** Leave wall up, finish photographing during closing session

**Challenge: Participants Staying Seated**

**Resistance:**
- Participants don't stand or engage with wall
- Energy is low after long afternoon

**Solutions:**
- **Explicit Direction:** "Everyone please stand now - we're doing this one standing"
- **Model Energy:** Facilitator stands first with enthusiasm
- **Physical Invitation:** Gesture toward wall, make eye contact, smile
- **Make It Easy:** Bring post-its to participants, then invite them to walk to wall to place them
- **Offer Choice:** "Would you like to place your post-its on the wall, or would you prefer someone else to place them for you?" (most will choose to do it themselves)

## Interactive Activity Format Guidance

### Overview

The workshop uses a variety of interactive formats to maintain energy and engagement throughout the 8-hour day. This section provides guidance on facilitating different activity formats: seated vs. standing work, individual reflection, group formation, sharing formats, energizers, and managing simultaneous activities.

### Mix of Seated and Standing Collaborative Work

**Rationale for Variety:**
- Sitting for 8 hours straight causes fatigue, disengagement, and physical discomfort
- Standing activities increase blood flow, energy, and focus
- Movement between formats creates natural transitions and reset points
- Different formats suit different learning styles and activity types

**Workshop Format Breakdown:**

**Seated Activities:**
- All presentations (10:00-11:15 AM, 11:30 AM-12:00 PM, 2:10-3:15 PM, 4:40-4:50 PM)
- Future-State Visioning group work (partially seated at tables, standing at flip charts)
- OKRs group work (partially seated at tables, standing at flip charts)
- Closing synthesis (4:50-5:00 PM)
- **Total Seated Time:** ~240 minutes (50% of workshop)

**Standing Activities:**
- Future-State Visioning flip chart creation and sharing (portions)
- OKRs flip chart creation (portions)
- Platform Requirements wall exercise (3:30-4:30 PM - fully standing)
- Gallery walks (optional)
- **Total Standing Time:** ~60-90 minutes (12-18% of workshop)

**Mixed Activities (Seated and Standing Options):**
- Participants choose whether to sit or stand during group discussions
- Some participants stand to stretch during long presentations

**Facilitating Seated Work:**

**Best Practices:**
- Encourage good posture (but don't lecture)
- Build in stretch breaks every 60-90 minutes
- Invite participants to stand during Q&A or discussions
- Use table groupings for collaboration

**Facilitating Standing Work:**

**Best Practices:**
- Give explicit direction: "We'll be standing for this activity"
- Model by standing first
- Ensure adequate floor space and comfort
- Offer seated alternatives for those with mobility limitations
- Keep standing activities to 30-60 minutes maximum

**Transitions Between Seated and Standing:**

See "Seated-to-Standing Transitions" in Room Configuration section for detailed guidance.

**Quick Tips:**
- Announce transition 5 minutes in advance
- Use transitions as bio-break opportunities (2-3 minutes)
- Clear floor space during transition
- Facilitator stands first to invite others

### Individual Reflection Periods Before Group Activities

**Why Individual Reflection Matters:**

- Prevents groupthink (everyone develops independent ideas first)
- Gives introverts time to process before speaking
- Creates equity (quiet voices have time to formulate thoughts)
- Produces better group discussions (more diverse ideas)

**How to Facilitate Individual Reflection:**

**Timing:**
- 5-10 minutes per reflection period
- Shorter for simple prompts (5 min), longer for complex thinking (10 min)
- Clearly announce start and end times

**Instructions:**
- "We'll start with 5 minutes of silent individual reflection"
- "Write down your thoughts - you don't need to share everything, but capture your top ideas"
- "No talking during this time - just thinking and writing"

**Tools to Provide:**
- Worksheets with prompts
- Sticky notes for individual brainstorming
- Blank paper and pens
- Access to earlier outputs (future-state visions, OKRs) for reference

**Facilitator Role:**
- Enforce silence (gently redirect if people start talking)
- Circulate quietly to observe progress
- Answer individual questions in whisper
- Give time warnings: "2 minutes left for individual reflection"
- Model reflection by writing your own notes

**Transitioning to Group Work:**

After individual reflection:
1. Announce: "Now turn to your group and share your top idea"
2. Set expectations: "Everyone should share at least one idea before deep discussion"
3. Use round-robin: "Go around the table - each person shares one idea"

**Individual Reflection Timing in Workshop:**

- **Future-State Visioning:** 5 minutes (9:05-9:10 AM)
- **OKRs Activity:** 10 minutes (1:05-1:15 PM)
- **Platform Requirements:** 15 minutes pain points + 15 minutes capabilities (individual silent post-it writing)

### Group Formation Techniques

**Counting Off Method (Random Mixing):**

**When to Use:** When you want cross-functional diversity or random grouping

**How to Do It:**
1. Determine number of groups needed (based on participant count)
2. Count off around room: "1, 2, 3, 1, 2, 3..." (for 3 groups)
3. All "1s" gather in one area, all "2s" in another, etc.

**Benefits:**
- Fast and simple
- Breaks up existing cliques
- Creates diverse perspectives in groups

**Drawbacks:**
- Random (might separate teammates from same organization)
- No control over group composition

**Topic-Based Grouping:**

**When to Use:** OKRs activity (groups focus on different platform engineering areas)

**How to Do It:**
1. Write focus areas on flip charts:
   - Developer Experience & Productivity
   - Platform Operations & Reliability
   - Security, Compliance & Governance
   - Business Impact & Cost Optimization
2. Ask participants to stand near the topic they're most interested in
3. Balance groups to 4-6 people each (split or combine as needed)

**Benefits:**
- Participants work on topics they care about (higher engagement)
- Groups develop specialized expertise in their area
- Outputs cover diverse aspects of platform engineering

**Drawbacks:**
- Uneven distribution (everyone wants same topic)
- May require facilitator adjustment to balance

**Organizational Grouping:**

**When to Use:** When participants represent different companies/organizations

**How to Do It:**
1. Group participants from same organization together
2. Each organization creates outputs relevant to their context

**Benefits:**
- Outputs are directly applicable to participants' work
- Builds team cohesion within organizations
- Easier to implement post-workshop action items

**Drawbacks:**
- Misses cross-organizational learning
- May reinforce existing organizational biases

**Self-Selected Grouping:**

**When to Use:** Low-stakes activities, creative exercises

**How to Do It:**
- "Form groups of 4-5 people - choose who you'd like to work with"

**Benefits:**
- Participants work with people they're comfortable with
- Fast (no facilitator orchestration needed)

**Drawbacks:**
- Can leave some participants out (socially awkward)
- May reinforce existing cliques

**Group Size Guidelines:**
- **3 people:** Minimum for diverse perspectives, can feel small
- **4-6 people:** Ideal range for most activities
- **7+ people:** Too large, quiet voices get lost

### Full Group Sharing Formats

**Report-Outs (Sequential Presentations):**

**Format:**
- Each group presents their work sequentially
- Time-limited (2-3 minutes per group)
- Q&A after each presentation (optional, 1-2 questions max)

**When to Use:**
- OKRs activity (groups present different OKR sets)
- Future-State Visioning (groups share creative visions)
- When outputs are distinct and complementary

**Facilitation:**
- Strict time management (use timer)
- Encourage different presenters from each group
- Capture themes across presentations for synthesis

**Pros:**
- Everyone hears all outputs
- Allows for Q&A and discussion
- Builds presentation skills

**Cons:**
- Time-intensive (7 minutes per group x 4 groups = 28 minutes)
- Can feel repetitive if outputs are similar
- Passive for non-presenting groups

**Gallery Walks:**

**Format:**
- Groups display outputs around room
- Participants tour all displays silently or with ambassadors
- Add sticky notes for feedback or dot stickers for voting

**When to Use:**
- When time is limited (faster than report-outs)
- When visual outputs are rich (flip charts, post-it clusters)
- When you want peer feedback

**Facilitation:**
- Display all outputs around room with space between
- Give 10-15 minutes for gallery walk
- Provide sticky notes or stickers for interaction
- Optional: Assign ambassadors to stay with their group's work

**Pros:**
- Time-efficient (simultaneous viewing)
- Active participation (walking, reading, feedback)
- Visual engagement

**Cons:**
- No verbal presentation or explanation
- Less discussion depth
- Requires wall space for display

**Teaching Back (Jigsaw Method):**

**Format:**
- Groups become "experts" on their topic
- Regroup participants into mixed groups
- Each person teaches their original group's work to new group

**When to Use:**
- When you want deep learning across all topics
- When time allows for complex regrouping
- Advanced facilitation (requires clear instructions)

**Facilitation:**
1. Original groups develop expertise (20 minutes)
2. Regroup: One person from each original group forms new group
3. Each person teaches their topic to new group (5 minutes each)

**Pros:**
- Deep learning (teaching reinforces understanding)
- Everyone engages actively
- Covers all topics comprehensively

**Cons:**
- Time-intensive
- Complex logistics (confusing regrouping)
- Not suitable for all workshop activities

**Popcorn Sharing (Informal, Voluntary):**

**Format:**
- Facilitator poses question
- Participants volunteer to share (raise hands)
- No formal structure or order

**When to Use:**
- Closing reflections
- Quick reactions or feedback
- Low-stakes sharing

**Facilitation:**
- Invite volunteers: "Who wants to share one key takeaway?"
- Don't force participation
- Keep shares brief (30 seconds to 1 minute)
- Affirm each share: "Thank you for that"

**Pros:**
- Flexible and organic
- Quick (only hear from those who want to share)
- Low pressure

**Cons:**
- Dominant voices may monopolize
- Quiet participants may not share at all
- Uneven coverage of content

### Energizer Activities and Movement Breaks

**Why Energizers Matter:**

- Combat afternoon energy slump (post-lunch, mid-afternoon)
- Reset attention after long presentations
- Build rapport and psychological safety
- Create positive emotional state

**When to Use Energizers:**

- **After Lunch (1:00 PM):** Quick energizer before OKRs activity
- **Mid-Afternoon (3:15 PM):** During break before Platform Requirements
- **Anytime Energy Drops:** If facilitator notices disengagement

**Types of Energizers:**

**1. Physical Movement:**
- Stand and stretch (30 seconds)
- Quick walk around room
- Partner high-fives or fist bumps
- Standing ovation for accomplishments

**Example:**
"Everyone stand up. Reach arms overhead. Take a deep breath. Shake out your hands. Now turn to someone next to you and give them a high-five. Let's get energized!"

**2. Quick Icebreakers:**
- Share one word describing how you're feeling
- Turn to neighbor and share most interesting thing you've learned
- Quick round of appreciations

**Example:**
"Turn to the person next to you. In 30 seconds each, share the most surprising thing you've learned so far today. Go!"

**3. Brain Teasers or Challenges:**
- Quick riddle or puzzle
- Creative prompt ("If this platform were an animal, what would it be?")
- Rapid-fire word association

**Example:**
"Quick challenge: Name as many CI/CD tools as you can in 30 seconds. Go!"

**4. Celebration and Acknowledgment:**
- Acknowledge progress made
- Celebrate completed activities
- Recognize contributions

**Example:**
"Look at all these flip charts you've created! Let's give ourselves a round of applause. You've done amazing work."

**Built-In Movement in Workshop:**

Rather than adding separate energizers, the workshop builds movement into activities:
- Standing flip chart work
- Gallery walks
- Wall-based post-it exercise
- Transitions between seated and standing

**Scheduled Breaks:**

- Mid-morning break (11:15-11:30 AM): 15 minutes
- Lunch (12:00-1:00 PM): 60 minutes
- Mid-afternoon break (3:15-3:30 PM): 15 minutes

**Quick Movement Breaks (2-3 minutes):**

Insert between sessions if needed:
- "Let's stand and stretch for a moment before the next session"
- "Take 2 minutes - stand up, grab water, then we'll continue"

### Managing Simultaneous Activities

**Scenario: Groups Working at Different Paces**

**Common Situations:**
- One group finishes flip chart in 15 minutes, another needs 30 minutes
- Some participants finish post-it brainstorming quickly, others need more time
- Uneven progress during OKRs activity

**Facilitation Strategies:**

**1. Extension Prompts for Fast Groups:**

If a group finishes early, give them additional challenges:

**For Future-State Visioning:**
- "Now prioritize - which 3 outcomes matter most?"
- "Add specific metrics to your vision - what numbers would success look like?"
- "Refine your flip chart - make it even more visual and clear"

**For OKRs Activity:**
- "Create a 4th OKR set"
- "Add more specific measurement details"
- "Identify potential obstacles to achieving these OKRs"

**For Platform Requirements:**
- "Keep adding more post-its - there's no limit"
- "Start thinking about how you'd prioritize these"

**2. Flexible Time Allocation:**

Build buffer time into activities:
- Schedule 25 minutes for group work but expect some groups to need 30
- Use 5-minute buffer for transitions
- Adjust on the fly: If most groups need more time, extend by 5 minutes

**3. Tiered Sharing:**

Fast groups present first:
- While fast groups present, slow groups have extra time to finish
- By the time last group presents, everyone is ready

**4. Gallery Walk Alternative:**

If groups are finishing at very different times:
- Skip report-outs entirely
- Do gallery walk format (groups finish when ready and display)
- Everyone tours at their own pace

**5. Pair Fast with Slow:**

If one group is struggling:
- Ask finished group to visit struggling group
- Peer coaching or collaboration
- "Group 1 is done - can you go see how Group 2 is doing and offer ideas?"

**Scenario: Simultaneous Activities at Tables and Walls**

**Example:** During Platform Requirements activity, some participants brainstorm at tables while others place post-its on wall

**Facilitation:**

1. **Clear Spatial Zones:**
   - Tables: Individual brainstorming and writing on post-its
   - Wall: Placing post-its and (later) clustering

2. **Phased Approach:**
   - Phase 1 (minutes 5-20): Everyone at tables writing pain points
   - Phase 2 (minutes 20-35): Everyone at tables writing capabilities
   - Phase 3 (minutes 35-50): Everyone at wall clustering
   - **Avoids confusion of simultaneous table/wall work**

3. **Traffic Flow:**
   - Establish pattern: "Write at your table, then walk to wall to place post-it, return to table"
   - Creates natural movement throughout activity
   - Prevents crowding at wall

**Scenario: Some Groups at Tables, Others at Walls**

**Not Recommended for This Workshop:**
The workshop doesn't require this level of complexity. All groups do same activity simultaneously.

**If Needed in Future:**
- Assign groups clearly: "Groups 1 and 2 at tables, Groups 3 and 4 at walls"
- Rotate halfway through: "Switch - Groups 1 and 2 to walls, Groups 3 and 4 to tables"

### Time Management for Different Paces

**Strategies:**

**1. Give Time Warnings:**
- "10 minutes left"
- "5 minutes left"
- "1 minute - start wrapping up"
- Creates urgency and helps groups pace themselves

**2. Minimum Viable Output:**

Set clear minimum expectations:
- "At minimum, complete 2 OKR sets"
- "Everyone should have at least 5 post-its on the wall"
- Groups aim for minimum first, then add more if time allows

**3. Accept Variable Depth:**

Not all groups need to produce same volume:
- Quality over quantity
- One group's 3 excellent OKRs is better than another's 5 mediocre ones

**4. Post-Workshop Completion:**

If time runs out:
- Capture what's been created
- Invite groups to continue work post-workshop
- "You can refine this further and send me updates"

**5. Facilitator Check-Ins:**

Circulate during group work:
- "How's it going? Do you need more time?"
- Gauge overall progress across all groups
- Make real-time decisions to extend or compress time

### Accessibility Considerations in Room Layout

**Wheelchair Access:**
- Pathways minimum 36 inches wide (48 inches preferred)
- Tables at standard height (28-30 inches) accommodate wheelchairs
- Leave space at each table for wheelchair positioning
- Wall activities: Ensure lower portions of wall are accessible (3-4 feet height)

**Sightlines:**
- Ensure all seats have clear view of screen (no obstructions)
- Stagger table rows if using classroom style
- Consider wheelchair users' sightlines (may be lower than standing facilitator)

**Hearing:**
- Room layout should minimize distance between facilitator and farthest participant
- Avoid overly large rooms or long rectangular shapes
- Consider sound-absorbing materials if room has echo

### Room Layout Adjustments During Day

**Minimal Changes Needed:**

The workshop design intentionally minimizes room reconfiguration to save time and reduce facilitator workload.

**Static Elements (Don't Move):**
- Tables
- Chairs (except during wall activity, pushed back slightly)
- Projector and screen

**Elements That Move:**
- Flip charts (from stands to walls for display)
- Flip chart stands (repositioned between activities if needed)
- Materials (distributed and collected)

**When Adjustments Happen:**
- During scheduled breaks (mid-morning, lunch, mid-afternoon)
- Facilitator or co-facilitator makes adjustments
- Participants can remain seated or on break
