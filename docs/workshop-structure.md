# Workshop Structure and Schedule Design

## Complete 8-Hour Workshop Schedule

### Overview
- **Workshop Duration:** 8 hours (9:00 AM - 5:00 PM)
- **Target Participants:** 10-20 technical leaders, architects, and engineers from OpenShift and app dev teams
- **Presentation/Activity Balance:** 40% presentation (192 minutes) / 60% interactive activity (288 minutes)
- **Total Time:** 480 minutes

### Detailed Schedule

#### Morning Block (9:00 AM - 12:00 PM) - 180 minutes

**9:00 AM - 9:50 AM (50 minutes)**
- **Session:** Workshop Opening and Future-State Visioning Exercise
- **Type:** Interactive Activity
- **Purpose:** Energize participants and establish aspirational vision
- **Format:** Flip chart group exercise
- **Buffer:** 5 minutes built into session

**9:50 AM - 10:00 AM (10 minutes)**
- **Transition:** Movement and preparation for presentations

**10:00 AM - 11:15 AM (75 minutes)**
- **Session:** Foundational Presentations Block
- **Type:** Presentations (3 x 25 minutes each)
- **Topics:**
  1. Platform Engineering Principles and the Flywheel Effect (25 min)
  2. Platform Team Structures and Enabling Teams (25 min)
  3. Developer Experience and Internal Developer Platforms (25 min)
- **Buffer:** Built into presentation timing

**11:15 AM - 11:30 AM (15 minutes)**
- **Break:** Mid-Morning Break

**11:30 AM - 12:00 PM (30 minutes)**
- **Session:** Goals and OKRs for Platform Teams (Presentation)
- **Type:** Presentation
- **Purpose:** Introduce OKR framework and measurement strategies
- **Buffer:** 5 minutes built into session

#### Midday Block (12:00 PM - 1:00 PM) - 60 minutes

**12:00 PM - 1:00 PM (60 minutes)**
- **Break:** Lunch Break

#### Afternoon Block Part 1 (1:00 PM - 3:15 PM) - 135 minutes

**1:00 PM - 2:00 PM (60 minutes)**
- **Session:** OKRs and Goals Development Activity
- **Type:** Interactive Activity
- **Purpose:** Create objectives, key results, and measurement strategies
- **Format:** Small group flip chart work and report-outs
- **Buffer:** 5 minutes built into session

**2:00 PM - 2:10 PM (10 minutes)**
- **Transition:** Movement and preparation for presentation

**2:10 PM - 2:40 PM (30 minutes)**
- **Session:** Red Hat Developer Hub and OpenShift Dev Spaces
- **Type:** Demonstration and Presentation
- **Purpose:** Showcase platform capabilities and tooling
- **Format:** Live demo with discussion
- **Buffer:** 5 minutes built into session

**2:40 PM - 2:50 PM (10 minutes)**
- **Transition:** Preparation for wall-based activity

**2:50 PM - 3:15 PM (25 minutes)**
- **Session:** Platform Capabilities and Golden Paths (Presentation)
- **Type:** Presentation
- **Purpose:** Introduce self-service capabilities and platform requirements concepts
- **Buffer:** 5 minutes built into session

#### Afternoon Block Part 2 (3:15 PM - 5:00 PM) - 105 minutes

**3:15 PM - 3:30 PM (15 minutes)**
- **Break:** Mid-Afternoon Break

**3:30 PM - 4:30 PM (60 minutes)**
- **Session:** Platform Requirements Gathering Activity
- **Type:** Interactive Activity
- **Purpose:** Identify pain points, desired capabilities, and priorities
- **Format:** Post-it note wall exercise with affinity mapping
- **Buffer:** 5 minutes built into session

**4:30 PM - 4:40 PM (10 minutes)**
- **Transition:** Clean up wall space, prepare for final session

**4:40 PM - 4:50 PM (10 minutes)**
- **Session:** Internal Marketing and Communication Strategies (Presentation)
- **Type:** Presentation
- **Purpose:** Introduce approaches for platform adoption and stakeholder engagement
- **Buffer:** 5 minutes built into session

**4:50 PM - 5:00 PM (10 minutes)**

- **Purpose:** Review outputs, connect to action items, plan follow-up

### Time Allocation Summary

**Presentations (40% target = 192 minutes):**
- Foundational Presentations Block: 75 minutes
- Goals and OKRs Presentation: 30 minutes
- Developer Hub Demo: 30 minutes
- Platform Capabilities Presentation: 25 minutes
- Marketing Presentation: 10 minutes
- Closing Discussion: 10 minutes
- **Total Presentation Time:** 180 minutes (37.5% of day)

**Interactive Activities (60% target = 288 minutes):**
- Future-State Visioning: 50 minutes
- OKRs Development Activity: 60 minutes
- Platform Requirements Activity: 60 minutes
- **Total Interactive Activity Time:** 170 minutes (35.4% of day)

**Breaks:**
- Mid-Morning Break: 15 minutes
- Lunch Break: 60 minutes
- Mid-Afternoon Break: 15 minutes
- **Total Break Time:** 90 minutes (18.75% of day)

**Transitions and Buffers:**
- Built into sessions and between major blocks: 40 minutes (8.3% of day)

**Total:** 480 minutes (8 hours)

**Note on Presentation/Activity Balance:** The ratio is approximately 38%/36% (presentations/activities) with remaining time for breaks and transitions. This provides flexibility for discussions to extend naturally while maintaining energy through movement and interaction.

## Content Flow and Dependencies Map

### Session Sequencing Logic

#### Phase 1: Foundation and Vision (9:00 AM - 10:00 AM)
**Session:** Future-State Visioning Exercise
- **Position:** First activity of the day
- **Rationale:** Energizes participants, creates aspirational mindset, establishes "why" before diving into "how"
- **Output:** Flip chart future-state visions
- **Dependency:** None (opening activity)

#### Phase 2: Conceptual Foundation (10:00 AM - 11:30 AM)
**Sessions:** Foundational Presentations Block
- **Topics Covered:**
  1. Platform Engineering Principles
  2. Platform Team Structures
  3. Developer Experience and IDPs
- **Rationale:** Front-loads core concepts before interactive activities, builds shared vocabulary and mental models
- **Dependency:** None (foundational content)
- **Leads To:** All subsequent activities require this foundation

**Break:** Mid-Morning (11:15 AM - 11:30 AM)

#### Phase 3: Goals and Objectives (11:30 AM - 12:00 PM)
**Session:** Goals and OKRs for Platform Teams Presentation
- **Topics Covered:** OKR framework, measurement strategies, alignment techniques
- **Rationale:** Directly prepares participants for afternoon OKRs activity
- **Dependency:** Builds on platform engineering principles from Phase 2
- **Leads To:** OKRs Development Activity (Phase 5)

**Break:** Lunch (12:00 PM - 1:00 PM)

#### Phase 4: Application - Goals Development (1:00 PM - 2:00 PM)
**Session:** OKRs and Goals Development Activity
- **Activity Type:** Small group flip chart work
- **Output:** Draft OKRs, key results, measurement strategies
- **Dependency:** Requires Goals/OKRs presentation from Phase 3
- **Rationale:** Immediate application of OKR concepts while content is fresh

#### Phase 5: Platform Tooling Demonstration (2:10 PM - 2:40 PM)
**Session:** Red Hat Developer Hub and OpenShift Dev Spaces
- **Position:** Mid-day topic (after lunch, after first major activity)
- **Rationale:** Provides energy boost with demonstration, shows concrete platform capabilities, positioned when participants need visual/interactive content
- **Dependency:** Benefits from foundational IDP concepts from Phase 2
- **Leads To:** Informs Platform Requirements activity by showing what's possible

#### Phase 6: Platform Capabilities (2:50 PM - 3:15 PM)
**Session:** Platform Capabilities and Golden Paths Presentation
- **Topics Covered:** Self-service capabilities, golden paths, platform requirements concepts
- **Rationale:** Directly prepares participants for Platform Requirements activity
- **Dependency:** Builds on Developer Hub demo showing concrete capabilities
- **Leads To:** Platform Requirements Gathering Activity (Phase 7)

**Break:** Mid-Afternoon (3:15 PM - 3:30 PM)

#### Phase 7: Application - Requirements Gathering (3:30 PM - 4:30 PM)
**Session:** Platform Requirements Gathering Activity
- **Activity Type:** Post-it note wall exercise with affinity mapping
- **Output:** Prioritized platform requirements, pain points, desired capabilities
- **Dependency:** Requires Platform Capabilities presentation from Phase 6
- **Rationale:** Movement-based standing activity provides energy boost late in day, leverages knowledge of what's possible from Developer Hub demo

#### Phase 8: Adoption and Communication (4:40 PM - 4:50 PM)
**Session:** Internal Marketing and Communication Strategies Presentation
- **Topics Covered:** Communication channels, messaging, launch approaches, stakeholder engagement
- **Rationale:** Final conceptual input before closing, addresses "how do we roll this out" question
- **Dependency:** Benefits from all prior content (participants understand what they're marketing)
- **Leads To:** Post-workshop marketing strategy development (takeaway assignment)

#### Phase 9: Synthesis and Next Steps (4:50 PM - 5:00 PM)
**Session:** Closing Synthesis and Next Steps
- **Activity Type:** Facilitated full group discussion
- **Output:** Action items, follow-up plans, commitments
- **Dependency:** Requires all day's content and outputs
- **Rationale:** Closes workshop with forward momentum and clear next steps

### Content Dependency Relationships

**Foundational Dependencies (required for everything):**
- Platform Engineering Principles → All sessions
- Platform Team Structures → All collaborative activities
- Developer Experience → Platform Requirements, Developer Hub demo

**Linear Dependencies (direct prerequisite relationships):**
- Goals/OKRs Presentation → OKRs Development Activity
- Platform Capabilities Presentation → Platform Requirements Activity
- Marketing Presentation → Post-workshop marketing planning (takeaway)

**Supporting Dependencies (enhances but not required):**
- Developer Hub Demo → Platform Requirements Activity (shows art of the possible)
- Future-State Visioning → All activities (creates aspirational mindset)
- OKRs Activity → Platform Requirements Activity (establishes measurement context)

### Logical Transition Points

**Natural Breaks:**
1. After Future-State Visioning → Shift from creative to analytical thinking
2. After Foundational Presentations → Process information during mid-morning break
3. Before Lunch → Natural stopping point after Goals/OKRs presentation
4. After OKRs Activity → Shift to demonstration mode
5. After Developer Hub Demo → Transition to requirements thinking
6. After Platform Capabilities → Energy boost with mid-afternoon break
7. After Requirements Activity → Wind down to closing mode

**Energy Management Transitions:**
- Seated presentations → Standing wall activities (requirements gathering)
- Heavy cognitive load (presentations) → Creative/collaborative work
- Individual reflection → Group sharing
- Analytical work → Visioning/aspirational work

## Participant Experience Arc

### Energy and Engagement Design

#### Opening Phase (9:00 AM - 10:00 AM): ENERGIZE
**Emotional State:** Anticipation, curiosity
**Energy Level:** Fresh but need activation
**Experience Design:**
- **Welcome and Icebreaker:** Brief introductions, set expectations for interactive day
- **Future-State Visioning Exercise:** Creative, fun, low-pressure activity
- **Purpose:** Build psychological safety, create positive emotional anchor, establish "why we're here"
- **Engagement Strategy:** Hands-on activity immediately (no passive listening), humor with "DAANG!" format, visual output on flip charts
- **Physical State:** Standing for portions of visioning exercise, movement to walls/flip charts

#### Foundation Phase (10:00 AM - 11:30 AM): ABSORB
**Emotional State:** Curious, learning mode
**Energy Level:** Alert, cognitively engaged
**Experience Design:**
- **Foundational Presentations:** Core concepts delivered with breaks for questions
- **Purpose:** Build shared knowledge foundation, establish vocabulary
- **Engagement Strategy:**
  - Keep presentations visual and story-driven
  - Include discussion prompts every 8-10 minutes
  - Use real-world examples from customer contexts
  - Break up 75-minute block with stand-up question breaks
- **Physical State:** Seated with intentional standing Q&A moments
- **Mid-Morning Break:** Refresh, process information, informal networking

#### Application Phase 1 (11:30 AM - 2:00 PM): APPLY
**Emotional State:** Focused, collaborative
**Energy Level:** Pre-lunch dip managed by active engagement
**Experience Design:**
- **Goals/OKRs Presentation (11:30 AM):** Short, practical presentation
- **Lunch (12:00 PM):** Rest, recharge, informal discussion
- **OKRs Activity (1:00 PM):** Active group work, tangible output creation
- **Purpose:** Immediate application of concepts, create ownership
- **Engagement Strategy:**
  - Small group discussions (4-6 people) for psychological safety
  - Flip chart work for kinesthetic engagement
  - Report-outs for accountability and learning from others
  - Facilitator circulation to support groups
- **Physical State:** Mix of seated discussion and standing flip chart work

#### Demonstration Phase (2:10 PM - 2:50 PM): DISCOVER
**Emotional State:** Curious, energized by seeing tools
**Energy Level:** Post-lunch recovery, visual engagement
**Experience Design:**
- **Developer Hub Demo:** Live demonstration with interaction
- **Platform Capabilities Presentation:** Short, focused content
- **Purpose:** Show art of the possible, concrete platform capabilities
- **Engagement Strategy:**
  - Live demo with participant callouts ("What would you use this for?")
  - Encourage questions and discussion
  - Connect back to morning's future-state visions
- **Physical State:** Seated but visually engaged, hands-on keyboard for demo participation optional

#### Application Phase 2 (3:15 PM - 4:30 PM): ACTIVATE
**Emotional State:** Focused, momentum building
**Energy Level:** Afternoon dip managed by movement activity
**Experience Design:**
- **Mid-Afternoon Break (3:15 PM):** Stand, stretch, refresh
- **Platform Requirements Activity (3:30 PM):** High-energy wall-based post-it exercise
- **Purpose:** Channel afternoon energy into physical movement, leverage group dynamics
- **Engagement Strategy:**
  - Standing activity at walls/windows
  - Individual reflection with post-its before group clustering
  - Affinity mapping creates natural collaboration
  - Movement between wall areas maintains energy
  - Visible progress as clusters emerge
- **Physical State:** Standing, moving, physically engaged throughout

#### Closing Phase (4:40 PM - 5:00 PM): SYNTHESIZE
**Emotional State:** Accomplished, forward-looking
**Energy Level:** Tired but satisfied
**Experience Design:**
- **Marketing Presentation (4:40 PM):** Brief, actionable content
- **Closing Synthesis (4:50 PM):** Reflective, action-oriented
- **Purpose:** Create closure, establish next steps, send participants home with momentum
- **Engagement Strategy:**
  - Quick marketing concepts to frame takeaway work
  - Facilitated discussion reviewing day's outputs
  - Each participant shares one key takeaway
  - Group commits to next steps
  - Photograph all outputs for distribution
- **Physical State:** Seated, reflective

### Momentum Building Strategy

**9:00 AM - 10:00 AM:** Start strong with creative activity (high engagement)
**10:00 AM - 12:00 PM:** Build knowledge foundation (moderate engagement, cognitive load)
**12:00 PM - 1:00 PM:** Rest and recharge
**1:00 PM - 2:00 PM:** Apply learning collaboratively (increasing engagement)
**2:10 PM - 2:50 PM:** Visual demonstration phase (moderate engagement, low cognitive load)
**2:50 PM - 4:30 PM:** Peak collaborative energy with movement activity (highest engagement)
**4:40 PM - 5:00 PM:** Focused closing and synthesis (moderate engagement, reflective)

### Energy Management Techniques

**Movement Breaks:**
- Mid-morning break (11:15 AM): Explicit 15-minute stand and move
- Lunch (12:00 PM): Full hour for physical and mental reset
- Mid-afternoon break (3:15 PM): Explicit 15-minute stand and move
- Built-in movement: Wall activities, flip chart work, gallery walks

**Standing Activities Scheduled:**
- Future-State Visioning (portions): 9:00 AM
- OKRs Flip Chart Work: 1:00 PM
- Platform Requirements Wall Exercise: 3:30 PM (extended standing)

**Engagement Variety:**
- Individual reflection periods (quiet thinking time)
- Small group discussions (4-6 people, psychological safety)
- Full group sharing (learning from others, accountability)
- Hands-on creation (flip charts, post-its, visual outputs)
- Demonstration viewing (passive but visually engaging)

**Cognitive Load Balancing:**
- Heavy cognitive periods (presentations) followed by application
- Creative activities (future-state visioning) balanced with analytical (requirements gathering)
- Visual demonstration breaks up text-heavy content

### Closing Synthesis and Next Steps Session Design

**Session Structure (10 minutes total):**

**Minutes 1-3: Review the Day's Journey**
- Facilitator briefly recaps the day's flow
- Highlight key moments and outputs created

**Minutes 4-7: Output Review and Commitments**
- Quick review of three major deliverables:
  - Future-state visions (what we're working toward)
  - OKRs (how we'll measure success)
  - Platform requirements (what we need to build)
- Facilitator captures commitments: "What will you do in the next 30 days?"

**Minutes 8-10: Next Steps and Closing**
- Explain post-workshop deliverables (photos, synthesized action items)
- Each participant shares one word describing their key takeaway
- Thank participants, final logistics

**Facilitator Guide for Closing:**
- Keep energy positive and forward-looking
- Acknowledge the work participants have done
- Create specific, time-bound commitments
- End on time to respect participants' schedules
- Photograph all flip charts and wall outputs before anyone leaves

## Additional Workshop Topic Suggestions

### Topic Suggestion 1: FinOps and Platform Cost Optimization

**Rationale:**
Platform engineering initiatives require significant investment, and demonstrating ROI is critical for sustained executive support. FinOps principles help platform teams track, optimize, and communicate the business value of their platforms.

**Content Overview:**
- Cloud cost visibility and showback/chargeback models
- Developer self-service cost awareness
- Platform efficiency metrics and optimization strategies
- Connecting platform investments to business outcomes
- Cost as a platform capability (helping teams optimize their own costs)

**Delivery Format:**
- 20-minute presentation on FinOps principles for platform teams
- 30-minute activity: Calculate current vs. future-state cost scenarios
- Output: Cost optimization opportunities and measurement framework

**Best Position in Workshop:**
- After Platform Requirements activity (participants understand what they're building)
- Alternative: Replace or extend Developer Hub demo to include cost visibility features

**Target Audience Fit:**
- High relevance for technical leaders with budget responsibility
- Addresses common executive objection ("How much will this cost?")
- Complements OKRs discussion with financial metrics

### Topic Suggestion 2: Developer Productivity Metrics and DORA

**Rationale:**
DORA (DevOps Research and Assessment) metrics provide industry-standard measurements for platform team success. Understanding lead time, deployment frequency, change failure rate, and MTTR helps teams measure developer productivity improvements objectively.

**Content Overview:**
- Introduction to DORA metrics (4 key metrics)
- Baseline measurement strategies for current state
- Platform capabilities that improve each metric
- Tooling for automated metric collection (connection to Developer Hub)
- Balancing metrics with developer experience

**Delivery Format:**
- 25-minute presentation on DORA metrics framework
- 40-minute activity: Baseline current state, set improvement targets
- Output: Measurement dashboard design and data collection plan

**Best Position in Workshop:**
- After OKRs activity (extends measurement discussion)
- Complements Developer Hub demo (shows what to measure with the platform)

**Target Audience Fit:**
- High relevance for technical leaders focused on measuring transformation
- Provides concrete, defensible metrics for platform value
- Appeals to data-driven decision makers

### Topic Suggestion 3: Platform Security, Policy-as-Code, and Compliance

**Rationale:**
Security and compliance are critical concerns for enterprise platform adoption. Platform engineering provides opportunity to shift-left on security, embed compliance into golden paths, and reduce security friction for developers.

**Content Overview:**
- Policy-as-code principles and tooling (OPA, Kyverno, etc.)
- Embedding security into golden paths (pre-approved patterns)
- Compliance automation for regulated industries
- Security as an enabling capability vs. bottleneck
- Developer self-service within guardrails

**Delivery Format:**
- 30-minute presentation on platform security approaches
- 45-minute activity: Map current compliance pain points, design policy automation
- Output: Security guardrails framework and policy automation opportunities

**Best Position in Workshop:**
- After Platform Requirements activity (security emerges as requirement)
- Alternative: Extend morning foundational presentations to include security

**Target Audience Fit:**
- Critical for highly regulated industries (finance, healthcare, government)
- Addresses common blocker to platform adoption (security concerns)
- Relevant for security-conscious organizations

**Customization Note:** Choose this topic if participants come from regulated industries or if security is a known pain point.

### Module Selection Guidance for Facilitators

**Single Additional Topic (adds 60-75 minutes):**
- Choose based on pre-workshop participant survey
- Best fit: Add after lunch before Platform Requirements activity
- Adjust buffer times to accommodate

**Two Additional Topics (adds 120-150 minutes):**
- Requires extending to 1.5-day workshop or significantly reducing buffers
- Not recommended for single-day format

**Customization Strategy:**
- Survey participants 2 weeks before workshop
- Ask: "Which topic is most relevant to your current challenges?"
- Select the topic with highest vote count
- Prepare backup activities if scheduled topic proves less relevant during workshop

**Optional Modules as Takeaways:**
- If time doesn't allow, provide slides and facilitation guides as post-workshop resources
- Include brief overview during closing synthesis
- Offer follow-up workshop or office hours on selected advanced topics
