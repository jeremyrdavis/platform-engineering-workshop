### Workshop Overview and Learning Objectives

**Workshop Purpose:**
This full-day interactive workshop helps technical leaders, architects, and engineers understand platform engineering principles and develop concrete action plans for building or enhancing their internal developer platforms. Through a mix of presentations and hands-on activities, participants create actionable deliverables including OKRs, platform requirements, and communication strategies.

**Who Should Attend:**
- Platform engineering team members and leaders
- DevOps engineers and SREs
- Application development team leads
- Technical architects
- Engineering managers
- Anyone involved in building or using internal developer platforms

**What Participants Will Learn:**

**Foundational Knowledge:**
- Platform engineering principles and the flywheel effect
- Platform team structures and enabling team patterns
- Developer experience and internal developer platform (IDP) concepts
- Golden paths and self-service capabilities
- OKR methodology for platform teams
- Internal marketing and stakeholder engagement strategies

**Practical Skills:**
- How to create compelling future-state visions for platform initiatives
- How to develop measurable OKRs aligned with business outcomes
- How to identify and prioritize platform requirements
- How to facilitate affinity mapping and collaborative decision-making
- How to communicate platform value to diverse stakeholders

**Actionable Outputs:**
By the end of the workshop, participants will have created:
1. **Future-State Vision:** Aspirational description of platform engineering success
2. **Platform OKRs:** Objectives, key results, and measurement strategies
3. **Platform Requirements:** Prioritized list of desired capabilities and pain points to address
4. **Communication Strategy:** Approaches for marketing platform internally (if time allows)
5. **Action Items:** 30-day and 90-day roadmap priorities

**Workshop Experience:**
Participants should expect:
- **Interactive Format:** Movement between seated presentations and standing collaborative activities
- **Collaborative Work:** Small group discussions, flip chart exercises, and post-it note brainstorming
- **Tangible Outputs:** Physical deliverables (flip charts, post-it clusters) photographed and digitized
- **Energy Management:** Scheduled breaks, movement activities, and variety to maintain engagement
- **Professional Documentation:** All outputs captured and provided to participants post-workshop

## II. Workshop Content and Delivery

### Complete Workshop Schedule

**Detailed Schedule with Session Summaries**
*For full session facilitation instructions, see the component guides referenced in each section.*

---

#### 9:00 AM - 9:50 AM: Future-State Visioning Exercise (50 minutes)
**Format:** Interactive flip chart activity
**Purpose:** Energize participants and establish aspirational vision
**Output:** Future-state vision flip charts

**Quick Summary:**
Small groups create "DAANG! [Company] [Achievement headline]" flip charts imagining their organization's platform engineering success. Groups list 8-12 specific outcomes (technical, cultural, business). Share via presentations and gallery walk.

**Facilitator Focus:**
- Energy and enthusiasm set tone for entire day
- Encourage bold, aspirational thinking (no wrong answers)
- Circulate during brainstorming to coach specificity
- Leave flip charts visible throughout day as reference

**Detailed Facilitation:** See Activities Guide, Activity 1

---

#### 9:50 AM - 10:00 AM: Transition and Preparation (10 minutes)
**Movement break and setup for presentations**

**Transition Script:**
"Fantastic visions! You've shown us where we want to go. Now let's build the foundation to get there. Please take a quick stretch break, and when you return, we'll dive into the core platform engineering concepts that will bring these visions to life."

---

#### 10:00 AM - 11:15 AM: Foundational Presentations Block (75 minutes)
**Format:** Three sequential 25-minute presentations
**Purpose:** Build shared knowledge foundation

**Topics Covered:**
1. **Platform Engineering Principles and the Flywheel Effect** (25 min)
   - What is platform engineering and why it matters
   - The platform engineering flywheel (better DX → higher adoption → more investment → better DX)
   - Value stream alignment and business impact
   - Common anti-patterns and pitfalls

2. **Platform Team Structures and Enabling Teams** (25 min)
   - Team Topologies and enabling team patterns
   - Platform team vs. application team responsibilities
   - Organizational models for platform teams
   - Cross-functional collaboration strategies

3. **Developer Experience and Internal Developer Platforms** (25 min)
   - What makes great developer experience
   - Components of an internal developer platform (IDP)
   - Self-service capabilities and golden paths
   - Metrics for measuring developer productivity

**Facilitator Focus:**
- Keep presentations visual and story-driven
- Include discussion prompts every 8-10 minutes
- Use real-world customer examples
- Build in stand-up question breaks during 75-minute block

**Detailed Facilitation:** See Core Sessions Guide, Morning Presentation Block

---

#### 11:15 AM - 11:30 AM: Mid-Morning Break (15 minutes)
**Rest, refresh, informal networking**

---

#### 11:30 AM - 12:00 PM: Goals and OKRs for Platform Teams (30 minutes)
**Format:** Presentation
**Purpose:** Introduce OKR framework before afternoon activity

**Topics Covered:**
- OKR methodology (Objectives and Key Results)
- Platform-specific OKR examples
- Measurement strategies and data sources
- Aligning platform OKRs with business objectives
- Common OKR pitfalls for platform teams

**Facilitator Focus:**
- Show concrete examples of platform OKRs
- Emphasize measurability and business alignment
- Preview afternoon OKRs activity to create anticipation
- End with bridge: "After lunch, you'll create your own OKRs"

**Detailed Facilitation:** See Core Sessions Guide, Goals and OKRs Presentation

---

#### 12:00 PM - 1:00 PM: Lunch Break (60 minutes)
**Rest, recharge, informal discussion**

**Facilitator Actions During Lunch:**
- Backup photos from morning to cloud storage
- Review afternoon session notes
- Set up materials for OKRs activity
- Eat and recharge your own energy!

---

#### 1:00 PM - 2:00 PM: OKRs and Goals Development Activity (60 minutes)
**Format:** Small group flip chart work
**Purpose:** Create measurable objectives and key results
**Output:** Draft OKRs with measurement strategies

**Quick Summary:**
Groups form by focus area (Developer Experience, Platform Operations, Security, Business Impact). Individual reflection followed by collaborative flip chart work. Each group develops 2-3 objectives with 3-5 key results each, plus measurement approach. Report-outs and facilitator synthesis.

**Facilitator Focus:**
- Push groups for specific, measurable key results (numbers, not vague "improvements")
- Coach distinction between objectives (qualitative) and key results (quantitative)
- Challenge groups to connect every key result to business impact
- Reference morning's future-state visions to inform objective selection

**Detailed Facilitation:** See Activities Guide, Activity 2

---

#### 2:00 PM - 2:10 PM: Transition (10 minutes)
**Movement break and preparation for demonstration**

**Transition Script:**
"Great work on your OKRs! Now you know what you want to achieve and how you'll measure success. Let's take a quick break, and then I'll show you some of the platform capabilities that can help you reach those goals."

---

#### 2:10 PM - 2:40 PM: Red Hat Developer Hub and OpenShift Dev Spaces (30 minutes)
**Format:** Live demonstration with discussion
**Purpose:** Show concrete platform capabilities and tooling

**Topics Covered:**
- Red Hat Developer Hub overview and value proposition
- Live demo of developer self-service workflows
- OpenShift Dev Spaces for cloud-based development environments
- Integration with platform engineering practices
- Connection to morning's concepts (golden paths, self-service)

**Facilitator Focus:**
- Keep demo engaging with "What would you use this for?" questions
- Connect capabilities back to participant pain points
- Show, don't just tell (live demo more engaging than slides)
- Invite participant questions throughout

**Detailed Facilitation:** See Core Sessions Guide, Developer Hub Demonstration

---

#### 2:40 PM - 2:50 PM: Transition (10 minutes)
**Preparation for Platform Capabilities presentation**

---

#### 2:50 PM - 3:15 PM: Platform Capabilities and Golden Paths (25 minutes)
**Format:** Presentation
**Purpose:** Introduce framework for platform requirements

**Topics Covered:**
- Self-service capabilities and what makes them valuable
- Golden paths and paved roads for common use cases
- Platform as a product mindset
- Identifying and prioritizing platform requirements
- Building vs. buying vs. leveraging open source

**Facilitator Focus:**
- Directly prepare participants for requirements activity after break
- Use examples from Developer Hub demo
- Preview post-it note exercise format to build anticipation

**Detailed Facilitation:** See Core Sessions Guide, Platform Capabilities Presentation

---

#### 3:15 PM - 3:30 PM: Mid-Afternoon Break (15 minutes)
**Rest, stretch, prepare for standing activity**

**Facilitator Actions During Break:**
- Set up post-it note stations with markers
- Designate wall areas (Pain Points, Desired Capabilities)
- Ensure adequate lighting on wall space
- Have camera ready for extensive photo capture
- Clear floor space for standing participants

---

#### 3:30 PM - 4:30 PM: Platform Requirements Gathering Activity (60 minutes)
**Format:** Standing post-it note wall exercise with affinity mapping
**Purpose:** Identify and prioritize platform capabilities
**Output:** Clustered, prioritized platform requirements

**Quick Summary:**
Full group exercise. Participants individually write pain points (pink post-its) and desired capabilities (blue post-its) on wall. Silent affinity mapping to cluster related items. Create theme labels. Dot voting for prioritization. Facilitator synthesis of top themes and priorities.

**Facilitator Focus:**
- This is highest-energy activity - embrace the movement and chaos
- Coach participants toward specific, concrete statements (not vague)
- Guide affinity mapping gently but don't dominate clustering
- Take extensive photos (close-ups of each cluster - post-it text is small)
- This activity produces most actionable workshop output

**Detailed Facilitation:** See Activities Guide, Activity 3

---

#### 4:30 PM - 4:40 PM: Transition (10 minutes)
**Clean up wall space, prepare for final sessions**

---

#### 4:40 PM - 4:50 PM: Internal Marketing and Communication Strategies (10 minutes)
**Format:** Brief presentation
**Purpose:** Frame platform adoption challenges and strategies

**Topics Covered:**
- Why platform adoption requires internal marketing
- Communication channels for different stakeholder groups
- Messaging that resonates (focus on "what's in it for them")
- Launch strategies and quick wins
- Measuring communication effectiveness

**Facilitator Focus:**
- Keep brief and actionable
- Connect to outputs created during day (what to communicate)
- Note that full marketing strategy activity is available as workshop extension
- Provide marketing as takeaway work for participants

**Detailed Facilitation:** See Core Sessions Guide, Internal Marketing Presentation

---

#### 4:50 PM - 5:00 PM: Closing Synthesis and Next Steps (10 minutes)
**Format:** Facilitated full group discussion
**Purpose:** Create closure and establish next steps

**Structure:**
- Brief recap of day's journey (2 min)
- Quick review of three major deliverables: visions, OKRs, requirements (3 min)
- Commitments: "What will you do in next 30 days?" (3 min)
- One-word takeaways from each participant (2 min)
- Thank you and logistics (post-workshop deliverables timeline)

**Facilitator Focus:**
- End on time to respect participants' schedules
- Keep energy positive and forward-looking
- Acknowledge the work participants have accomplished
- **CRITICAL:** Ensure all flip charts and post-its are photographed before anyone leaves!

**Detailed Facilitation:** See Core Sessions Guide, Closing Synthesis Session

## III. Workshop Execution

### Session-by-Session Facilitation Flow

**This section provides the moment-to-moment facilitation flow for the entire workshop day.** Use this as your roadmap during the workshop itself.

---

**8:30 AM - 9:00 AM: Pre-Workshop Arrival and Setup**

**Your Actions:**
- Greet participants warmly at door as they arrive
- Direct participants to tables, encouraging mixing across organizations/teams
- Distribute participant agendas
- Make informal small talk to build rapport and ease nervousness
- Observe participant energy levels and social dynamics
- Do final tech checks (projector, timer, camera)

**Set the Tone:**
- Be warm, welcoming, and energetic
- Model the collaborative spirit you want to see
- Build psychological safety through casual conversation

---

**9:00 AM - 9:05 AM: Workshop Opening**

**Your Script:**
"Good morning everyone! Welcome to the Platform Engineering Workshop: Building Your Internal Developer Platform. I'm [Your Name], and I'm excited to spend the day with you exploring how platform engineering can transform your development teams' experience and productivity.

Before we dive in, a few logistics:
- We'll be together until 5:00 PM with breaks mid-morning, lunch at noon, and mid-afternoon
- This is an interactive workshop - expect to move, collaborate, and create tangible outputs
- Please silence phones and laptops during presentations, but feel free to use them for notes
- We have a 'parking lot' flip chart here for questions that come up but are off-topic - we'll address those during breaks or at the end
- Bathrooms are [location], refreshments are [location]

Now, introductions. Let's go around the room quickly - share your name, role, and one challenge you're hoping we'll address today related to platform engineering or developer experience. [Facilitate quick intros - 1 minute per person max]

Great! I heard themes around [synthesize common challenges]. We'll address many of these today. Let's dive in!"

**Time Management:** Keep opening to 5 minutes maximum. Resist urge to over-explain agenda - they have it in writing.

---

**9:05 AM - 9:50 AM: Future-State Visioning Exercise**

**Follow detailed facilitation from Activities Guide, Activity 1**

**Key Moments:**
- 9:05-9:10: Energetic introduction to "DAANG!" format (model enthusiasm)
- 9:10-9:25: Groups brainstorming and creating flip charts (circulate and coach)
- 9:25-9:45: Group presentations (2-3 min each, keep time strictly)
- 9:45-9:50: Facilitator synthesis and bridge to foundations

**Your Energy Focus:**
This sets the tone for the entire day. Be energetic, playful, and encouraging. Celebrate creative thinking.

---

**9:50 AM - 10:00 AM: Transition**

**Your Script:**
"Fantastic visions! You've shown us where we want to go - faster onboarding, true CI/CD, self-service everything, developers who are excited to work here. These are powerful aspirations.

Now, how do we get there? The next 75 minutes will give you the conceptual foundation - the platform engineering principles, team structures, and developer experience insights that make these visions achievable.

Let's take a quick 10-minute stretch break. When you return, grab coffee or water, and we'll dive into the foundation."

**Your Actions:**
- Leave future-state vision flip charts visible
- Set up projector for presentations
- Queue up first presentation slide
- Use restroom yourself if needed
- Greet participants as they return

---

**10:00 AM - 11:15 AM: Foundational Presentations Block**

**Follow detailed facilitation from Core Sessions Guide, Morning Presentation Block**

### Managing Energy and Engagement

**Energy management is critical for 8-hour workshop success.** Use these techniques:

**Physical Energy:**
- **Movement Activities:** Schedule standing activities (Requirements at 3:30 PM) when energy dips
- **Scheduled Breaks:** Honor breaks strictly - participants need them
- **Variety:** Alternate seated presentations, table work, and wall activities
- **Stretch Breaks:** Invite participants to stand and stretch during long presentations

**Cognitive Energy:**
- **Front-Load Heavy Content:** Foundational presentations in morning when fresh
- **Application After Absorption:** Activities follow relevant presentations
- **Visual Breaks:** Use demo and visual content post-lunch
- **Creative Activities:** Visioning and collaborative work provide cognitive relief from lectures

**Emotional Energy:**
- **Start Strong:** Energetic opening and fun first activity create positive momentum
- **Celebrate Progress:** Acknowledge outputs and progress throughout day
- **Humor:** Use appropriate humor to lighten mood
- **End Positively:** Close with accomplishment, not exhaustion

**Participant Engagement Techniques:**

**During Presentations:**
- Ask "show of hands" questions every 10 minutes
- Use "think-pair-share" for quick discussions
- Call on participants for perspectives (but gently)
- Connect abstract concepts to concrete examples from their context

**During Activities:**
- Circulate actively among groups (don't sit)
- Ask coaching questions to deepen thinking
- Celebrate creative or novel approaches publicly
- Keep time visible and give regular warnings

**When Energy Drops:**
- Call a spontaneous 2-minute stretch break
- Shift to interactive Q&A instead of lecture
- Move to standing activity earlier than planned
- Inject humor or energy into facilitation

**When Participants Disengage:**
- Notice body language (arms crossed, looking at phones)
- Adjust facilitation: less talking, more interaction
- Check in during break: "How's the pace? What's landing?"
- Be willing to skip or abbreviate content to preserve engagement

### Troubleshooting Common Challenges

**Comprehensive troubleshooting guidance is in each component guide, but here are top challenges:**

**Challenge: Running Behind Schedule**
- **Prevention:** Give time warnings, keep strict time on report-outs
- **In Moment:** Reduce report-out time (3 min → 2 min per group), skip gallery walks, shorten closing synthesis
- **Accept:** Better to finish strong at 5:10 PM than rush everything poorly

**Challenge: Running Ahead of Schedule**
- **In Moment:** Extend Q&A during presentations, allow longer sharing time, add deeper discussion prompts
- **Use Buffers:** Built-in transition buffers can absorb extra time
- **Avoid:** Don't end early - participants expect full day

**Challenge: Participant Asks Complex Question Off-Topic**
- **Response:** "Great question - let me add that to the parking lot and we'll address it [later/post-workshop]."
- **Follow Through:** Actually address parking lot items during breaks or closing

**Challenge: Group is Stuck During Activity**
- **Response:** Sit with group, ask prompting questions from Discussion Prompt Bank
- **Offer Examples:** Point to example outputs or suggest starting point
- **Reframe:** "If the template isn't working, try a different approach"

**Challenge: One Person Dominating Discussion**
- **Redirect:** "Thanks for that. What do others think?"
- **Round-Robin:** "Let's hear one idea from everyone before we discuss"
- **Private Coaching:** Pull person aside during break: "Help me make sure everyone shares"

**Challenge: Post-Its Falling Off Wall**
- **Prevention:** Test wall surface before workshop, have painter's tape ready
- **Fix:** Use tape to create sticky surface or tape post-its to wall

**Challenge: Camera Fails or Photos Are Lost**
- **Prevention:** Backup photos to cloud during lunch
- **Backup:** Use participant smartphone, borrow device, delay cleanup until photos captured
- **Recover:** Use scribe notes to reconstruct outputs if needed

**Challenge: Participant Cynicism or Resistance**
- **Acknowledge:** "These challenges are real. What would need to change?"
- **Reframe:** Focus on what's in their control, start with small wins
- **Don't Debate:** Validate concern and move forward

**For complete troubleshooting guidance, see Appendix B: Troubleshooting Index**

**Room Configuration for Different Activities:**

**Presentations (10:00 AM, 11:30 AM, 2:10 PM, 2:50 PM, 4:40 PM):**
- Participants seated at tables
- Focus toward front projector screen
- Facilitator at front near screen
- Flip charts moved to side (not blocking view)

**Flip Chart Activities (9:00 AM, 1:00 PM):**
- Groups gather around flip chart stands (standing or seated)
- Flip chart stands distributed around room
- Groups work simultaneously at different stands
- Facilitator circulates among groups

**Post-It Wall Activity (3:30 PM):**
- All participants standing at wall/window space
- Tables pushed back to create floor space
- Post-it note stations set up on tables within reach
- Wall clearly visible from across room for synthesis

**Transitions:**
- Participants move physically between configurations
- Use transitions as movement breaks
- Facilitator guides participants: "Please gather at flip chart #1" or "Everyone to the wall for post-its"
